 Coug AI Build Challenge: Kickoff
 Friday, October 23, 2026 · The Commons, Pullman · WSU MIS Advisory Board

  Your challenge: build an AI-powered tool that turns a team's own project materials into a clear, honest
  picture of where the project stands and what to do next. Aim for something that names a specific task,
  person, date, and reason, in language a busy teammate can absorb in ten seconds.


 The scenario
 Meet Team X, six students in UNIV 497: Interdisciplinary Capstone Studio, a fictional 15-week course. Their
 sponsor is Palouse Harvest Food Share, a food bank whose volunteer coordinator, Dana Whitfield, wants a better
 way for volunteers to sign up for shifts. The team is designing that solution. Teammates go by their major.
 For this kit, today is Wednesday, October 21, 2026, Week 9. The team just held its weekly meeting. Their next
 graded milestone, a working prototype and sponsor demo, is due Friday, October 30. Everyone has been busy,
 notes have been written by whoever remembered, and the task list was last touched whenever someone felt like it.
 Nobody is quite sure where things stand.
 Teammate                                Role                        Teammate                               Role
 MIS Student                             Team lead                   Marketing Student                      Sponsor liaison
 Comp Sci Student                        Tech / prototype lead       Communication Student                  Writing / docs lead
 Digital Tech Student                    Design and UX               Psychology Student                     Research lead

 Team X is your test data, not your target. Your tool should work just as well for a marketing campaign, a lab report,
 or a policy brief.

 What's in your packet
 File                                     What it is
 00_README.txt                            Kit overview, the team roster, and the date to treat as "today." Read this first.
 01_Project_Brief_UNIV497.pdf             The instructor's brief: deliverables, due dates, grading rubric, and course policies.
 02_Meeting_Notes/                        Notes from the team's weekly meetings, written by different teammates in different
                                          styles and formats.
 03_TeamX_tasks.xlsx                      The team's task list, kept the way student teams really keep them.
 04_team availability.xlsx                Class schedules, work hours, and dates people are away.
 05_Week10_Update/                        One week later: new meeting notes and an updated task list. Save this until your tool
                                          works on Weeks 1 to 9.

 The materials don't always agree with each other, just like a real team's. A good tool notices, and says how sure it
 is.

 Pick a direction
 Choose one and go deep, or bring an original idea that tackles the same problem.
  • The tracker that builds itself. Reconstruct the plan the team is actually working from, using the brief and
    meeting notes.
  • The early warning system. Find what's about to go wrong before the next deadline, and say what to do about it
    this week.
  • The fair share view. Show how work is really distributed and help the team start an honest, respectful
    conversation.
  • The team check-in coach. Run the weekly check-in: a short status, the three things that matter most, and
    questions for the next meeting.

 How to get started
  • Spend the first 15 minutes choosing one insight. One team, one moment, one insight. Don't build a whole
    project management platform.
  • Read the packet like a teammate would. Skim everything before you prompt anything. Note what surprises
    you.
  • Do dates and counts in code, not in the AI. Let the AI read, connect, and explain. A demo that gets a due date
    wrong is over.
  • Test constantly on Team X, then try the Week 10 update. If you can, try a group project of your own (with your
    teammates' permission).
  • Stop building at 1:30 pm and rehearse your five-minute demo.

 How you'll be evaluated
 Judges score five areas. They are looking for real, useful work, not polish.
 Area                                 Weight       In short
 Working demonstration                30%          Something real runs live on the sample project.
 Team impact                          25%          The guidance is specific enough that a team would act on it this week.
 Works for any project                15%          Nothing is hard-coded to Team X, their course, or their major.
 Responsible AI thinking              15%          Protects teammates' dignity, shows its sources, and knows when to defer to
                                                   people.
 Presentation                         15%          Five clear minutes: the problem, the demo, the honest limitations.

 Bonus: show your tool working on one of your own real group projects, with your teammates' permission.

 Ground rules
  • Use only fictional or permitted data. No real grades, student records, or personal information.
  • Support the team, don't judge it. Flag risks without assigning blame. Ask yourself: would a teammate be
    comfortable seeing this about themselves?
  • Keep humans in charge. Your tool should say when it's unsure and when a conversation is the right next step.
